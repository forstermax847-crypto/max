Breast symptoms

Breast lump assessment

Assessment of a new lump or any other breast change, with Miss Samantha Williams at BMI The Ridgeway Hospital, Swindon. Examination, imaging and any biopsy are usually done in one visit.

Most breast lumps are benign: cysts, fibroadenomas or ordinary glandular tissue make up most of what is seen in clinic. The only way to be sure is a proper assessment, and the sooner it is done, the sooner you have an answer. Appointments for new symptoms are prioritised.

Symptoms that should be checked

  • A new lump or an area of thickening in the breast or armpit
  • A change in the size, shape or outline of one breast
  • Dimpling, puckering or a change in the texture of the skin
  • A nipple that has newly turned inward, changed position, or developed a rash or crusting
  • Discharge from the nipple, particularly from one side only or blood-stained
  • Persistent pain in one part of the breast
  • Any concern about breast implants, including a change in shape or new firmness

You do not need to be sure something is wrong to book. Wanting a lump looked at is reason enough.

What happens at the appointment

Assessment follows the triple assessment standard used in NHS breast clinics.

1. History and examination

Miss Williams asks what you have noticed and when, about your general health, medications and family history, and examines both breasts and the lymph nodes in the armpits and neck. A chaperone is always offered. You are welcome to bring someone with you.

2. Imaging

Usually an ultrasound scan, a mammogram, or both, depending on your age and the findings. Under 40, the breast is normally assessed with ultrasound first; from about 40, a mammogram is usually added. Imaging is done by specialist breast radiologists at the hospital, and wherever possible on the same day.

3. Biopsy, if needed

If the imaging shows something that needs further characterising, a small sample is taken with a needle under local anaesthetic, guided by ultrasound. This takes a few minutes. Most people feel pressure rather than pain. Cysts can often be drained at the same time.

Results

Imaging results are explained before you leave. Biopsy results are usually available within about a week, and Miss Williams goes through them with you in person or by phone, whichever you prefer. If everything is benign, you will be told what, if anything, to keep an eye on. Benign lumps such as fibroadenomas can be monitored or removed if they are large, growing or you would rather they were gone.

If the result is cancer, you hear it from the surgeon who will treat you. Your case is discussed at a multidisciplinary team meeting, and Miss Williams explains the options for surgery and any other treatment.

Other reasons patients attend

  • Breast pain and cyclical breast changes
  • Nipple inversion or discharge
  • Breast asymmetry
  • Checks of existing breast implants, including suspected rupture or capsular contracture
  • A family history of breast cancer and questions about your own risk
  • A second opinion on a previous finding or recommendation

Risks

Examination and imaging carry no meaningful risk. A needle biopsy can cause bruising and tenderness for a few days; infection or significant bleeding are rare. Occasionally a biopsy has to be repeated if the sample is not sufficient.

Questions patients ask

Do I need a GP referral?

No, if you are paying for the appointment yourself. If you want to use private medical insurance, most insurers ask for a GP referral before authorising cover, so contact them first.

Will I get results on the same day?

Imaging results, yes. If a biopsy is needed, the laboratory result usually takes about a week and is given to you in person or by phone.

What should I bring?

Any previous breast imaging reports or letters, a note of your medications, your insurance details and authorisation if applicable, and a list of questions. Wearing separates makes examination easier.

I am in my twenties. Could it still be cancer?

Breast cancer is uncommon under 30 and the great majority of lumps at this age are benign, but any new lump should be assessed. Ultrasound works well in younger, denser breasts.

Can I be assessed if I have implants?

Yes. Ultrasound and mammography can both be done with implants in place, and Miss Williams can assess the implants themselves. Mention them when you book.

Is the assessment covered by insurance?

Assessment of a new symptom is usually covered by private medical insurance, subject to your policy and pre-authorisation. Self-pay prices are given when you book.
