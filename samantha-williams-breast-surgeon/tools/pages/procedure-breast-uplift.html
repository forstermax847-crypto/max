Cosmetic breast surgery

Breast uplift

Mastopexy lifts and reshapes breasts that have dropped or lost fullness after pregnancy, breastfeeding, weight loss or with age. Performed by Miss Samantha Williams at BMI The Ridgeway Hospital, Swindon.

An uplift removes excess, stretched skin, tightens and reshapes the breast tissue, and moves the nipple to a higher position. It does not add much volume. If you also want fuller breasts, an implant can be placed in the same operation (an augmentation-mastopexy); if you want them smaller, the uplift is combined with a reduction. Working out which of these you actually need is the main purpose of the first consultation.

Who it suits

An uplift is worth considering if the nipples point downward or sit at or below the crease under the breast, the upper part of the breast looks empty, the areolae have stretched, or one side has dropped more than the other. The degree of droop determines which technique and scar pattern is appropriate.

Results are best if your weight is stable, you have finished breastfeeding (ideally six months or more before surgery) and you do not smoke. An uplift does not stop the breast changing with age, gravity, weight and pregnancy, and some settling of the result over the years is normal.

The consultation

Miss Williams examines you, assesses skin quality, breast volume and nipple position, and discusses what will achieve the shape you describe. The main decisions are:

  • Uplift alone, or with an implant. An implant restores upper fullness that an uplift alone cannot. Doing both in one operation is common but carries a somewhat higher chance of needing an adjustment later than either procedure on its own, so staging them is sometimes the better choice.
  • Scar pattern. Around the areola only for mild droop; around the areola plus a vertical line for moderate droop; an anchor pattern with a scar in the fold for more significant droop or excess skin.
  • Areola size. Stretched areolae can be reduced in the same operation.

You leave with a written plan, at least two weeks to consider it, and the option of a second consultation.

The operation

Under general anaesthetic, excess skin is removed and the breast tissue is reshaped and supported internally, often moving tissue upward to restore fullness. The nipple and areola are repositioned while remaining attached to their blood and nerve supply. If an implant is planned, it is placed at this stage. Incisions are closed with dissolvable stitches under the skin. Depending on the extent of surgery you go home the same day or the next morning.

Recovery

Discomfort is usually mild to moderate for the first week and controlled with simple painkillers. A supportive bra is worn day and night for about six weeks. Most people return to desk work after about two weeks and to full exercise at about six weeks. The breasts look high and tight at first; they settle into their final shape over three to six months. Scars fade over 12 to 18 months. Follow-up is usually at one week, six weeks and six months.

Risks

  • Permanent scars, which usually fade well but are occasionally thicker or wider than hoped.
  • Changes in nipple sensation, common at first and sometimes permanent. Very rarely, the blood supply to the nipple is compromised.
  • Wound-healing problems, particularly where scars meet, and more likely in smokers.
  • Asymmetry, or a shape that settles differently from what was expected, occasionally needing revision.
  • Recurrent droop over time, especially with larger breasts, weight change or further pregnancies.
  • Breastfeeding may be affected.
  • If an implant is used, the implant-related risks described on the augmentation page also apply, and the combined operation has a higher revision rate than either alone.
  • Bleeding, infection, blood clots and anaesthetic risks.

Questions patients ask

Do I need an implant as well as an uplift?

Only if you want more volume, particularly in the upper breast. An uplift alone reshapes and raises what you have. Miss Williams will explain what each option would realistically achieve.

How visible are the scars?

Scars around the areola blend into the change of colour at its edge; vertical and fold scars are covered by a bra or bikini. All scars are red and noticeable at first and fade over a year or more. How well you scar is partly individual.

How long does an uplift last?

Many years, but not indefinitely. Breasts continue to change with age, gravity, weight and pregnancy. A stable weight and supportive bras help the result last.

What is the difference between an uplift and a reduction?

Both lift and reshape the breast with similar scars. A reduction also removes a significant amount of breast tissue to make the breast smaller and lighter. Which is right depends on whether you are happy with your current size.

Can I have an uplift after major weight loss?

Yes; it is one of the most common reasons for the operation. Miss Williams usually recommends waiting until your weight has been stable for several months.
