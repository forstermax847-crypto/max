From First Call to Recovery

The consultation path

  1. 01
    Consultation

    An unhurried first conversation — examination, imaging where relevant, and a candid discussion of what surgery can and can't do for you.

  2. 02
    Your plan, and time to reflect

    A written plan specific to your anatomy and goals. For cosmetic surgery you will always have a cooling-off period of at least two weeks before anything is booked.

  3. 03
    Procedure day

    Performed by Miss Williams at BMI The Ridgeway Hospital, with a consultant anaesthetist and a clear aftercare protocol.

  4. 04
    Recovery

    Structured follow-up — typically at one week, six weeks and six months — with direct access to the team if anything concerns you in between.

Before You Get in Touch

Questions patients often ask

Do I need a GP referral?

Not to book a self-pay consultation — you can refer yourself directly. If you're using private medical insurance, most insurers require a GP referral letter before they'll authorise cover, so it's worth checking with them first.

I've found a lump and I'm worried. How quickly can I be seen?

Contact Tracy, Miss Williams's medical secretary, directly — fast-track appointments with same-day imaging and results can usually be arranged for urgent concerns, rather than waiting for a routine slot.

How long is there between a cosmetic consultation and surgery?

At least two weeks. In line with General Medical Council guidance on cosmetic procedures, you will always be given a cooling-off period to reflect on the written plan, ask further questions and change your mind without pressure. Many patients have a second consultation in that time.

Will Miss Williams perform my surgery herself?

Yes. You'll see her at every appointment, from first consultation through to follow-up, and she performs the procedure herself with a consistent theatre team — not a colleague or trainee on the day.

If I have breast cancer, will I automatically need a mastectomy?

Not necessarily. Techniques such as therapeutic mammoplasty and chest wall perforator flap reconstruction can allow larger cancers to be removed while conserving the breast. Whether this is suitable depends on the size, position and type of cancer, and will be discussed honestly at consultation.

Is cosmetic breast surgery covered by insurance?

Generally, no — procedures chosen for personal, cosmetic reasons are usually self-pay. Reconstructive and medically necessary procedures are typically covered, subject to your policy and insurer pre-authorisation.

Do you only see private patients?

No. Miss Williams is a Consultant Oncoplastic Breast Surgeon at Great Western Hospital as well as seeing private patients at BMI The Ridgeway Hospital, Swindon.

Can I come to Miss Williams for a second opinion?

Yes — second opinions on a diagnosis, a proposed treatment plan, or the outcome of previous surgery are a regular part of her private practice.
