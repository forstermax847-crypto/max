Breast lump assessment

A new lump or change deserves a specialist answer quickly, not weeks of worry. Fast-track consultation with Miss Samantha Williams, with same-day imaging and results wherever possible, at BMI The Ridgeway Hospital, Swindon.

Finding a lump or noticing a change in your breast is frightening, and waiting for an appointment makes it worse. Most breast lumps turn out to be benign — cysts, fibroadenomas or normal glandular tissue — but the only way to know is a proper assessment. Miss Williams offers a one-stop clinic: examination, imaging and, if needed, a biopsy in a single visit, with imaging results discussed with you the same day.

Symptoms worth having checked

You do not need to be sure something is wrong to book an assessment — "I just want it looked at" is reason enough. Changes that should always be assessed include:

  • A new lump or an area of thickening in the breast or armpit
  • A change in the size, shape or outline of one breast
  • Dimpling, puckering or a change in the texture of the skin
  • A nipple that has newly turned inward, changed position, or developed a rash or crusting
  • Discharge from the nipple, particularly if it is from one side only or blood-stained
  • Persistent pain in one part of the breast
  • Any concern about breast implants, including a change in shape or new firmness

What happens at the appointment

Assessment follows the same "triple assessment" standard used in NHS breast clinics, delivered by one consultant who then stays with you whatever the result.

1. Consultation and examination

Miss Williams will listen to what you have noticed and when, ask about your general health and any family history, and examine both breasts and the lymph nodes in your armpits and neck. A chaperone is always offered. You are welcome to bring someone with you.

2. Imaging

Depending on your age and the findings, this is usually an ultrasound scan, a mammogram, or both — under 40 the breast is typically assessed with ultrasound first; over 40 a mammogram is usually added. These are performed by specialist breast radiologists at the hospital and, wherever possible, on the same day.

3. Biopsy, if needed

If the imaging shows something that needs characterising, a small sample is taken with a needle under local anaesthetic, guided by ultrasound. It takes a few minutes and most people describe pressure rather than pain. Cysts can often be drained at the same time, which relieves both the lump and the worry.

Getting your results

Imaging results are explained to you before you leave. Biopsy results are usually available within about a week and Miss Williams will arrange to go through them with you in person. If everything is benign, you will be reassured, told what — if anything — to keep an eye on, and discharged or offered follow-up as appropriate. Benign lumps such as fibroadenomas can be monitored or removed if they are large, growing or you would simply rather they were gone.

If the result is cancer, you will hear it from the surgeon who will treat you, with time, honesty and a clear plan. Miss Williams's case will be discussed at a multidisciplinary team meeting and she will guide you through the options for surgery and any other treatment — the same consultant throughout.

Other reasons patients come to this clinic

  • Breast pain (mastalgia) and cyclical breast changes
  • Nipple inversion or discharge
  • Breast asymmetry or concerns about breast development
  • Checks of existing breast implants, including suspected rupture or capsular contracture
  • A family history of breast cancer and questions about your own risk
  • A second opinion on a previous finding or recommendation

Risks

Examination and imaging carry no meaningful risk. A needle biopsy can cause bruising and tenderness for a few days; infection or significant bleeding are rare. Occasionally a biopsy needs repeating if the sample is not sufficient.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a GP referral to be seen?

No — you can book directly as a self-paying patient. If you want to use private medical insurance, most insurers ask for a GP referral before authorising cover, so contact them first. Either way, call or message Tracy Greenside and she will arrange the earliest suitable appointment.

Will I get my results on the same day?

Imaging results, yes — Miss Williams discusses them with you before you leave. If a biopsy is needed, the laboratory result usually takes around a week and is given to you in person or by phone, whichever you prefer.

What should I bring?

Any previous breast imaging reports or letters, a note of your medications, your insurance details and authorisation if applicable, and a list of questions. Wearing separates makes examination easier. You are welcome to bring a partner, friend or relative.

I'm in my twenties — can it still be cancer?

Breast cancer is uncommon under 30, and the great majority of lumps at this age are benign, but any new lump should still be assessed. Ultrasound is very effective at characterising lumps in younger, denser breasts.

Can I be assessed if I have implants?

Yes. Ultrasound and mammography can both be performed with implants in place, with adapted techniques, and Miss Williams can also assess the implants themselves. Tell the team you have them when you book.

Is a breast lump assessment covered by insurance?

Diagnostic assessment of a new symptom is usually covered by private medical insurance, subject to your policy terms and pre-authorisation. Self-pay prices are provided clearly when you book.
