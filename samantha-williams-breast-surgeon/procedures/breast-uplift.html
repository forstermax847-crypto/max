Cosmetic Breast Surgery

Breast uplift

Mastopexy lifts and reshapes breasts that have dropped or deflated after pregnancy, breastfeeding, weight loss or simply time — restoring a shape that sits where it used to. Performed by Miss Samantha Williams at BMI The Ridgeway Hospital, Swindon.

A breast uplift removes excess, stretched skin, tightens and reshapes the breast tissue, and moves the nipple to a higher, more natural position. It does not add significant volume: if you also want fuller breasts, an implant can be placed at the same time (an augmentation-mastopexy), and if you want them smaller the uplift is combined with a reduction. Knowing which of these you actually need is the most important part of the first consultation.

Is an uplift right for you?

An uplift is worth considering if your nipples point downward or sit at or below the crease under the breast, the upper part of the breast looks empty, the areolae have stretched, or one side has dropped more than the other. The degree of droop (surgeons grade it) determines which technique and scar pattern is appropriate, and Miss Williams will show you exactly what each would involve.

As with all cosmetic breast surgery, results are best if your weight is stable, you have finished breastfeeding (ideally six months or more beforehand), and you do not smoke. It is also important to understand that an uplift turns the clock back — it does not stop it. Breasts continue to change with age, gravity, weight and pregnancy, and some settling of the result over the years is normal.

Your consultation

Miss Williams will examine you, assess skin quality, breast volume and nipple position, and talk candidly about what will achieve the shape you describe. Key decisions include:

  • Uplift alone, or with an implant — an implant restores upper fullness that an uplift alone cannot. Combining the two in one operation is common, but carries a somewhat higher chance of needing an adjustment later than either procedure on its own; staging them is sometimes the safer choice.
  • Scar pattern — around the areola only for mild droop; a "lollipop" (around the areola plus a vertical line) for moderate; an anchor pattern adding a scar in the fold for more significant droop or excess skin.
  • Areola size — stretched areolae can be reduced during the same operation.

You will leave with a written plan, at least two weeks to reflect, and the option of a second consultation.

The operation

Under general anaesthetic, excess skin is removed and the underlying breast tissue is reshaped and supported internally, often moving tissue upward to restore fullness. The nipple and areola are repositioned while remaining attached to their blood and nerve supply. If an implant is planned it is placed at this stage. Incisions are closed with dissolvable stitches beneath the skin. Depending on the extent of surgery you will go home the same day or the next morning.

Recovery

Discomfort is usually mild to moderate for the first week and controlled with simple pain relief. A supportive bra is worn day and night for around six weeks. Most people return to desk work after about two weeks and resume full exercise at around six weeks. Early on the breasts look high and tight — this is intentional, and they settle into their final shape over three to six months. Scars fade over 12–18 months. Follow-up is typically at one week, six weeks and six months.

Risks and things to consider

  • Scars — permanent, usually fading well, occasionally thicker or wider than hoped.
  • Changes in nipple sensation — common initially; sometimes permanent. Very rarely, nipple blood supply is compromised.
  • Wound-healing problems, particularly where scars meet; more likely in smokers.
  • Asymmetry or a shape that settles differently from what was expected, occasionally needing revision.
  • Recurrent droop over time, especially with large or heavy breasts, weight change or further pregnancies.
  • Breastfeeding may be affected.
  • If an implant is used — all the implant-related considerations described on the augmentation page apply, and the combined operation has a higher revision rate than either alone.
  • General risks — bleeding, infection, clots and anaesthetic risks.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an implant as well as an uplift?

Only if you want more volume, particularly in the upper breast. An uplift alone reshapes and raises what you have. Miss Williams will show you what each option would realistically achieve so you can decide with clear expectations.

How visible will the scars be?

Scars around the areola blend into the colour change at its edge; vertical and fold scars are hidden by a bra or bikini. All scars are red and noticeable at first and fade over a year or more. How well you scar is partly individual and cannot be guaranteed.

How long does a breast uplift last?

Many years, but not forever. Breasts continue to age and respond to gravity, weight changes and pregnancy. Maintaining a stable weight and wearing supportive bras helps the result last.

What is the difference between an uplift and a reduction?

Both lift and reshape the breast and use similar scars; a reduction also removes a significant amount of breast tissue to make the breast smaller and lighter. Which is right depends on whether you are happy with your current size.

Can I have an uplift after significant weight loss?

Yes — it is one of the most common reasons for the operation. Miss Williams will usually recommend waiting until your weight has been stable for several months so the result is planned on the breast you will keep.
