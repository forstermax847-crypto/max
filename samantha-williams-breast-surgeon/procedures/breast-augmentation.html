Cosmetic Breast Surgery

Breast augmentation

Breast enlargement with implants, sized to your frame rather than to a number — so the result looks like it was always yours. Performed by Miss Samantha Williams, Consultant Oncoplastic Breast Surgeon, at BMI The Ridgeway Hospital, Swindon.

Breast augmentation uses an implant to add volume, improve shape or restore fullness that has been lost after pregnancy, breastfeeding or weight loss. It is the most common cosmetic breast operation in the UK — and the one where honest, individual advice about size and proportion makes the biggest difference to how natural the result looks.

Is augmentation right for you?

Women consider augmentation for many reasons: breasts that have always felt small for their frame, volume that has deflated after children or weight change, or a noticeable difference between the two sides. The best candidates are in good general health, at a stable weight, not currently pregnant or breastfeeding, and clear about what they want to change — and what they want to keep.

Augmentation adds volume; it does not lift. If your nipples sit low or the breast has dropped, an implant on its own can make this more obvious. In that case Miss Williams may recommend an uplift, either on its own or combined with an implant, and will explain the trade-offs of each honestly.

Your consultation

The first appointment is unhurried. Miss Williams will take a full history, examine and measure you, and ask what you are hoping for — in your words, not cup sizes. You will try implant sizers in a bra to see how different volumes sit on your frame, and discuss:

  • Size and proportion — including what your chest width and existing tissue can realistically carry without looking artificial or causing problems later.
  • Implant type — round or anatomical (teardrop), surface finish, and the profile that suits your shape.
  • Placement — in front of or partly behind the chest muscle ("dual plane"), which affects how natural the upper breast looks, particularly in slimmer patients.
  • Incision — most often in the fold beneath the breast, where the scar is well hidden.

You will leave with a written plan. For all cosmetic surgery there is a cooling-off period of at least two weeks before anything is booked, in line with General Medical Council guidance, and a second consultation is offered if you would like one. If a smaller implant will look better on you than the one you had in mind, Miss Williams will tell you — several of her patients have written about exactly that.

The operation

Augmentation is performed under general anaesthetic with a consultant anaesthetist and usually takes between one and one and a half hours. A pocket is created either behind the breast tissue or partly beneath the pectoral muscle, the implant is positioned, and the incision is closed with dissolvable stitches beneath the skin. You will wake in a supportive post-surgical bra and most patients go home the same day or the following morning.

In line with national guidance, the details of your implants are recorded in the Breast and Cosmetic Implant Registry, and you will be given an implant card for your own records.

Recovery

Expect the breasts to feel tight, swollen and tender for the first week, settling steadily after that. Simple pain relief is usually enough. Most patients return to a desk-based job within one to two weeks and can drive again once they can comfortably perform an emergency stop — typically one to two weeks (check your insurer's policy). You will wear a supportive bra day and night for around six weeks and avoid lifting, upper-body exercise and swimming during that time.

Implants sit high and feel firm at first; they soften and settle into a more natural position over two to three months, and the final result is usually judged at around six months. Scars are pink and slightly raised initially and fade over 12–18 months. Follow-up appointments are typically at one week, six weeks and six months, with the team available in between.

Risks and things to consider

Augmentation is a well-established operation, but it is still surgery, and implants are not lifetime devices. Miss Williams will go through the following with you in detail before you decide:

  • General surgical risks — bleeding or haematoma, infection, delayed wound healing and reactions to anaesthetic. Smoking significantly increases these risks.
  • Changes in nipple or breast sensation — usually temporary, occasionally permanent.
  • Capsular contracture — the scar tissue that naturally forms around an implant can tighten over time, making the breast feel firm or look distorted, and may need further surgery.
  • Rippling, visible edges or implant rotation — more likely in slimmer patients with less natural tissue, which is one reason placement and size matter.
  • Asymmetry — no two breasts are identical before surgery, and small differences can remain or appear afterwards.
  • Implant rupture or wear — implants can fail over time. Many women will need further surgery to replace or remove them at some point in their lives.
  • Breast implant-associated anaplastic large cell lymphoma (BIA-ALCL) — a rare cancer of the immune system linked mainly to textured implants, and the symptoms some women report as "breast implant illness". Both are discussed openly at consultation.
  • Breastfeeding and screening — most women can breastfeed after augmentation, but it cannot be guaranteed. Implants can make mammograms harder to read, so you should always tell the screening unit you have them.

None of this is meant to put you off. It is meant to make sure that when you decide, you decide with the full picture.

Frequently asked questions

How do I choose the right implant size?

Not from a cup size, which varies between brands, but from your measurements, your existing breast tissue and the look you describe. Trying sizers in a bra at consultation gives a good sense of volume. Miss Williams will advise on the range that will look natural on your frame — and be honest if the size you had in mind is likely to look obviously augmented.

Will my implants look natural?

That is the aim of every augmentation Miss Williams performs. A natural result depends on choosing a size in proportion to your chest, placing the implant correctly for your tissue, and — where the breast has dropped — being honest that an uplift may be needed as well. Results vary between individuals and no outcome can be guaranteed.

Do breast implants need to be replaced?

Not on a fixed schedule, but they are not designed to last a lifetime. Many women have further surgery at some point because of changes in the breast, capsular contracture, rupture or simply a change in preference. You should plan on the basis that you may need another operation in the future.

Can I breastfeed after augmentation?

Most women can. The incision in the breast fold and placement beneath the muscle both help to preserve the milk ducts and nerves, but breastfeeding cannot be guaranteed after any breast surgery. If you plan to have children soon, it may be worth discussing timing at consultation.

Will implants affect breast cancer screening?

Implants can obscure part of the breast on a mammogram, so the screening unit uses extra views when they know you have them. Always tell them. As a breast cancer surgeon, Miss Williams will also talk you through how to stay breast-aware after augmentation.

How soon can I exercise?

Gentle walking from the first few days; light lower-body exercise from around two weeks; and running, swimming, upper-body work and anything high-impact from about six weeks, once Miss Williams has checked you at follow-up. Everyone heals at a different pace, so this is guidance rather than a rule.
