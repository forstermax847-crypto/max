Recovering from breast reduction surgery

What each stage of recovery looks like, from the first night in hospital to the scars a year later.

Miss Samantha Williams, Consultant Oncoplastic Breast Surgeon. About 6 minutes to read.

Draft for clinical review. This article was prepared for Miss Williams to review and approve before publication.

Everyone heals at their own pace, and the timings below are typical rather than guaranteed. Most women recovering from breast reduction follow a similar path, and most say afterwards that the relief of weight was felt almost immediately, before the soreness had settled.

The first few days

You usually stay in hospital for one night. The breasts feel tight, sore and heavy with dressings, and you will feel tired, which is the anaesthetic and the body's response to surgery rather than anything to worry about. Regular simple painkillers are usually enough; stronger ones are available for the first day or two if needed. You go home in a soft, supportive post-surgical bra, worn day and night for about six weeks. Sleep propped up on pillows, on your back, for the first couple of weeks. It reduces swelling and keeps pressure off the wounds.

Move about the house from day one. Short, gentle walks help circulation and reduce the risk of clots. Keep your arms below shoulder height and avoid lifting anything heavier than a kettle, including small children and pets.

The first week

Swelling and bruising peak around day three to five and then ease. Dressings are checked at the first follow-up, usually about a week after surgery. Some numbness of the skin and nipples is normal at this stage. Showering is fine once the dressings allow; baths and swimming wait until the wounds have fully healed. Many patients need very little pain relief by the end of the first week.

Weeks two and three

Most women return to desk-based or light work after about two weeks. You can drive once you could do an emergency stop without hesitation, usually around two weeks, but check your insurer's policy. Gentle lower-body exercise such as walking and a stationary bike is fine; nothing that bounces or works the chest. Small areas of delayed healing, most often where the vertical scar meets the fold under the breast, are not unusual at this stage and are managed with simple dressings. Mention anything that concerns you.

Six weeks

Most restrictions lift after the six-week review. You can usually return to full exercise, including running, swimming, the gym and upper-body work, building up gradually, and change from the surgical bra to a supportive everyday bra without underwiring for a further few weeks. Swelling has largely settled, though the breasts are still firmer and sit higher than they eventually will.

Three to six months

The breasts soften and settle into their final shape, dropping slightly into a more natural position. Sensation often continues to return over this period, sometimes with tingling or shooting feelings as nerves recover. The six-month review is usually when the result is assessed.

Scars over the first year

Reduction scars run around the areola, vertically down the breast and usually along the fold beneath it. They are red, raised and noticeable for the first few months, then flatten and fade to pale lines over 12 to 18 months. Keeping them out of the sun helps, and massaging them with a plain moisturiser once fully healed. Silicone gel or tape can be used from about three weeks if you tend to form thick scars. Some people scar more than others and this cannot be predicted fully, but almost everyone finds the scars fade more than they feared and are covered by a bra or bikini.

When to get in touch sooner

  • Increasing pain, swelling or firmness on one side, particularly in the first few days
  • Redness spreading from a wound, discharge from a wound, or a temperature
  • Any change in the colour of the nipple
  • Calf pain or breathlessness

You will have the team's contact details before you go home.

Keeping the result

A reduction is permanent, but breasts still change with life. Significant weight gain, pregnancy and breastfeeding can all alter them again. A stable weight and a supportive bra for exercise are the two things that most help the result last.
